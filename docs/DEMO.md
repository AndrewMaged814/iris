# Demo: “Iris, what’s happening in my market?”

**Target: 2 minutes 50 seconds.** This is a working-demo production plan, not a completed
recording. The video follows an actual SME owner planning the week with Iris.

## The product story

> The owner runs the business. Iris helps them keep up with the market: gathering relevant
> signals across competitors, connecting the findings, investigating what they mean and
> helping the owner choose a practical response.

Use an Egyptian skincare store with **two product families**: sunscreen and moisturizers.
The owner wants to know where to focus this week, rather than react to individual alerts.

Show three competitor businesses, six to eight useful public pages and a native connected
catalog read. Use Infinity Clinic Pharma, Nut Botanicals and Cairo Drop as starting candidates;
confirm relevant products before recording and replace poor matches. Distinguish brand,
retailer and marketplace when comparing them. These names are a research roster, not verified
current findings or an already-installed multi-store watchlist.

## The opening

A short shot of the owner’s existing competitor tabs and their store catalog.

**Owner’s message:**

> Iris, I’m planning this week for my skincare store. Give me a market briefing for sunscreen
> and moisturizers. Look at Infinity and two other relevant stores. What should I pay
> attention to, and where do we stand?

**Narration:**

> “You’re running the store. Who’s keeping up with the market?”

Then show Iris researching and sharing an actual useful interim finding while further
needed reads continue. One simple overlay identifies sources checked; it is an editorial
overlay, not a new dashboard.

## What the market briefing should contain

The viewer should see three concise insights rather than a list of disconnected pages:

1. **Competitor activity:** what is currently listed or promoted, and which changes are
   supported by dated history.
2. **Cross-competitor comparison:** relevant alternatives grouped around the owner’s sunscreen
   and moisturizer, with product/offer differences preserved.
3. **Owner relevance:** the most important threat, opportunity or unresolved question for
   this store, based on its actual products and goal.

A useful visual is a small editorial table: **competitor / observed signal / why it matters /
source and date**. Populate it from the actual run. Avoid fake competitor scores or a dashboard
claim. The business conclusion comes from Iris, not from a predetermined script.

## Go deeper on Infinity

**Owner:**

> How is Infinity doing right now across the products you checked? Is this one isolated offer
> or a broader campaign? What do we know, and what might explain it?

Needed evidence may include:

- Two relevant official product pages, not one headline.
- An official campaign or collection page to understand scope and terms.
- Saved observation history, with its actual coverage period.
- A relevant announcement, public policy or external context page **only when it answers
  a material question**.

A source starting point is the
[Infinity gel page](https://infinityclinicpharma.com/ar/products/naturals-sun-screen-gel-spf-50-oily-skin).
Other pages must be discovered/read and kept with the run. The
[fixed retrieval fixture](../tools/fixtures/retrieval_check.json) also provides a Nut Botanicals
product lead. A URL is a lead, not proof of its current price.

“How is Infinity doing?” can establish public activity. It cannot establish sales growth,
financial health or strategy from listings alone. An official explanation is useful evidence;
a supported hypothesis can be useful if labeled.

## The connection that makes the demo memorable

The target moment is a connection across sources that changes the owner’s priority.

For example, **if the actual evidence supports it**, Iris might connect:
a rival’s multi-buy campaign, another rival’s unavailable comparable item, and the owner’s
available single-item product. That could support testing an availability message for
single-item shoppers. It does not prove customer demand will transfer.

Other valid outcomes include noticing a specific own-listing gap, identifying a stronger
relevant competitor, or finding that the supposed opportunity is unconfirmed. Choose the
actual supported finding after the rehearsal. Do not force a promotion, a shortage or a
“smart” motive into the footage.

**Owner:**

> My goal is to get more useful enquiries this week. I can spend ten minutes on an
> existing-channel post. What should I focus on, and why?

Costs or a quick calculation may support a proposed move when needed. Keep them within
the investigation; a standalone arithmetic exchange is not the demo’s main event.

## Turn intelligence into a response

**Owner:**

> Turn that recommendation into a short Egyptian Arabic message for my customers.
> Use the facts we confirmed.

Review the actual draft for unverified claims, product benefits and availability.
Then the owner chooses:

> I’ll use that message myself this week. Remember the move and how we’ll review it.
> Don’t publish anything for me.

Open a fresh native conversation:

> What did we decide for this week, and what should I track?

Show actual recall of the chosen move and success measure. An optional later results review
can live in a longer walkthrough; a 2–3 minute film does not need simulated sales to explain
the product. Do not make a suggestion appear to be an owner commitment.

## Closing narration

> “Iris brings your market into the conversation: what’s happening, what matters to your
> business, and what to do next. Built on Hermes, connected through Composio, and inspectable
> through real traces.”

Show one measured manual-vs-Iris task result when available, including correction time.
If no measurement exists, say “SME impact measurement in progress.”
End with **“Your AI market watcher.”**

## Rehearsal and recording

1. Prepare a separate, owner-restricted demo profile and authorized demo catalog.
   Label demo product facts. Retain Luna, Hermes and native Composio.
2. Verify three relevant businesses and six to eight useful sources across both product
   families. More tabs only help when they add evidence.
3. For any change-over-time claim, provide genuine dated observations. The active owner
   watchlist currently has one competitor; expanding the demo’s watch coverage is not
   already completed. Current research can cover additional businesses without historical claims.
4. Run the whole conversation once. Retain replies, source evidence, dates, tool paths,
   timing, token usage and cost status. Check product matches and source conflicts manually.
5. Fix a factual failure before featuring it; rerecord the workflow rather than replacing
   a live answer with a scripted one.
6. Record one real scheduled check/alert if it has relevant evidence. Show it briefly in
   the briefing scene; otherwise present on-demand research and existing scheduling clearly.
7. Capture the trace belonging to the demonstrated run, sanitize it, and test judge access.

The evidence capture is an operator workflow; it does not add a service or alter Iris’s tools.

## Done when

- [ ] The recorded investigation covers multiple businesses, product families and source types.
- [ ] The briefing connects findings and prioritizes something useful for this owner.
- [ ] The Infinity follow-up reuses evidence and adds genuinely needed context.
- [ ] Dates, coverage, hypotheses and demo catalog facts are clear.
- [ ] The draft is grounded and fresh-session memory works.
- [ ] The trace/timing belongs to the run; missing cost remains unknown.
- [ ] Measured impact and judge access are verified or explicitly pending.
- [ ] The final recording lasts 2–3 minutes.
