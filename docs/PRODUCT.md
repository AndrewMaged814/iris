# Iris — AI growth scout for small online stores

Iris turns public competitor evidence and an owner's catalog into one relevant move, a useful
draft and a weekly follow-up. Telegram is the workspace; Egyptian Arabic and English are the
working languages. [Research and source limits](research/BUSINESS_IMPACT.md).

## Owner problems to validate

- Repeated competitor checking takes attention away from serving customers.
- A price or promotion alert still leaves comparison, margin judgment and copywriting to do.
- Suggested actions disappear into chat without a record of what was used or measured.
- Product listing gaps can be observed and corrected, even when their sales effect is unknown.

These are pilot hypotheses, not a claim that all SMEs have the same needs. Iris currently fits
one small Shopify retailer per profile; she is not a general CRM or a campaign publisher.

## The working loop

1. Owner approves competitors; Iris establishes a baseline.
2. Daily checks detect structured catalog changes. Iris reads the relevant owner product and
   competitor evidence, explains match limits and offers one feasible action.
3. On request, Iris drafts from verified product facts or reviews one product's listing gaps.
4. Owner chooses or launches a move. Hermes memory records its status, date and success measure.
5. Sunday brief reviews evidence and one due owner-confirmed action. Owner's reply updates the
   result; missing measurements remain unknown. A simulated exercise remains simulated.

History and suggestions are different: one snapshot cannot establish a quiet week, current
stock cannot prove demand, and gross sales cannot establish profit or causal uplift.

## Pilot definition of done

One real owner receives a correct relevant proactive alert, obtains a usable draft, chooses
and completes one move, and reports measured task time plus a sourced business outcome. Verify
the actual Telegram conversation and follow-up; isolated model runs alone are insufficient.

Record the evidence without customer-level data:

| Measure | Evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| Checking/drafting time saved | Same bounded task, owner's usual/manual time and actual Iris-assisted time; date and method |
| Actionable opportunity | Fresh relevant evidence, named owner product, feasible move and owner acceptance |
| Action used | Owner confirms publication/edit/use, with date; re-read store changes when possible |
| Business outcome | Period, enquiries/orders/gross revenue and source; costs and comparison for profit or uplift claims |
| End-to-end reliability | Inbound owner turn, correct product/tool evidence, final response delivery and persisted follow-up |

The current synthetic catalog and simulation prove parts of the workflow. They do not satisfy
this real-owner pilot requirement. [Current evidence](STATUS.md).

Completing the wider goal requires all three: an owner-validated feasible revenue opportunity,
measured time saved on a repeated task, and the complete owner conversation/action/follow-up
working through Telegram. Research, attractive documentation, synthetic results or green unit
tests alone cannot satisfy those gates. Profit and incremental-revenue claims additionally
need costs and a defensible comparison; otherwise report the narrower observed result.

## Boundaries and next candidates

Four tools, three scripts; Hermes supplies memory, scheduling, sessions, vision and delivery.
All stores remain read-only. No customer messaging, campaign publishing or automatic price changes.

Prioritise the next changes from observed failures: promotion evidence in scheduled checks,
bundle versus component availability, actionable copy that saves editing, and reliable review of
chosen actions. Add order/analytics access only when a pilot needs it and the owner authorises it;
catalog data cannot substitute for sales data.
