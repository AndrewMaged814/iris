# Market briefings

A briefing answers where the owner's business stands across the requested market scope.
Its useful result is a connected market picture, followed by a priority when requested.

1. Set coverage from the owner's question: businesses, product families and relevant dimensions
   such as availability, positioning, campaigns or policies. Track each requested business/family
   as verified, partially verified or inaccessible; retain useful non-price evidence.
2. Read the confirmed connected catalog once for the needed products, sizes, descriptions,
   prices and availability. Read the setup skill for native app access. A zero inventory count
   and a purchasable flag mean availability needs confirmation; neither settles it alone.
3. Reuse exact supplied or watched URLs. Search for each requested business and product
   family, open the matching pages, and compare them with the owner's catalog. A sunscreen
   collection does not finish a face-moisturizer search. Read the pages that carry the
   prices, sizes and terms you will cite. A reworded search for the same business and product
   is not a new fact; open a page already found instead.
4. Extract relevant product and campaign/collection evidence. Keep observed positioning as
   retailer claims, not independently verified benefits. Read history only to establish changes.
   When price remains uncertain, keep supported positioning, format and campaign findings.
   A price dispute blocks that price verdict, not the rest of a researched business's briefing.
   Calculate only quantities you will report, using one batch of confirmed-price rows per family;
   keep unresolved-price products in the briefing as qualitative evidence. Reuse the calculation.
   A search result is a lead, not a verified product fact. Every quoted public price, size,
   availability and offer term must come from a successful page read at that exact cited URL.
   If a promising alternative appears late in search, open it before quoting it or leave it out.
   Once a business/family has useful read evidence, investigate only gaps that could change
   this answer. An unresolved price need not delay a qualitative briefing or enquiry priority.
5. Connect findings by product family across businesses, then to the owner's catalog and goal.
   State the concrete pattern and its limits: what is shared, what differs and what matters
   for this store. A current listing establishes presence, not a launch or recent change.
   Do not invent a shared routine, bundle or strategy to make the opening sound insightful.
   If no shared pattern is verified, lead with the strongest supported finding instead.
6. Reply with the main market finding, short grouped sections and source/date links for the
   material claims. When stock affects availability or the priority, use the inventory count on that exact
   product/variant record; never borrow another product's count. If that record is 0 and
   still marked purchasable, availability is unresolved. Missing stock stays unknown. Name a rival's spray, gel, cream or lotion as that product's format.
   Call it different from the owner's product only
   when the owner's format is written in the evidence. Include each requested business, or the precise unresolved coverage gap.
   About 180–250 words is suitable for a two-family briefing; completeness takes priority over
   an arbitrary word count. Explain where the owner stands using verified dimensions only.
   Compare known attributes with known attributes. If your catalog says only "sunscreen", report
   the rival's cream/gel format and your unspecified format without claiming they differ.
   Before sending, check every material fact against its exact product/variant and successful
   source read. Remove search-only numbers and unchecked URLs. Keep one concise finding per
   business/family; avoid repeating all caveats in the opening and closing.
   Attribute own facts to the connected catalog in plain text. Only add an own-product link
   when its exact record returned a nonempty public URL. `onlineStoreUrl: null` means no
   returned public link; never turn a store domain plus product handle into a source citation.
   Apply this check to follow-up recommendation footers as well as the initial briefing.
7. For a requested priority, use Choose the response. Keep the recommendation separate from
   copy: write customer-facing text after the owner asks for a draft. Compare possible responses
   against the owner's stated goal and effort; give a review measure without predicting results.
   Finish a briefing with its findings and priority. Mentioning a planned post or allowing drafts
   in a rehearsal describes the owner's context; a separate drafting request starts the copy step.
