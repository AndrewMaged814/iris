# Explaining a competitor's move

Use for "why", "is this normal", "clearing stock" and the business relevance of that explanation.

1. Read `market_changes` for observed history and its coverage. Read the known official
   announcement/current campaign and relevant product examples. Search for an explanation
   only when those sources leave a specific lead to investigate; read that lead before widening.
2. Distinguish the observed move from its reason. An official explanation is a retailer's stated
   reason. Holidays, timing and rivals' moves support hypotheses; they do not prove motive.
   Deep discounts and "only10left" do not establish clearance. Missing clearance evidence
   does not establish routine promotion. State the scope actually checked.
3. Offer a hypothesis only with a relevant supporting fact and the missing evidence that would
   distinguish it from another explanation. One observation cannot show recurrence; category
   demand cannot establish this store's sales. With no distinguishing evidence, motive is unknown.
4. Explain why the observed campaign could matter to the owner, conditional on its actual terms.
   A motive question needs no own-price/stock investigation. "Should I care?" asks for relevance;
   recommend attention to the confirmed offer's terms rather than inventing a price recommendation.
   An explicit "should I change my price?" follows Choose the response in the main skill.
5. Stop after explaining the observed move, the supported/unknown reason and its relevance.
   A follow-up asking for proof reuses the evidence. List what each source supports, without
   redoing setup or reading unrelated catalog facts.

Structured snapshots omit page promotion terms. A current offer absent from those snapshots
does not prove when it began. Describe a limited period of price/stock observations accurately.
If a later comparison becomes necessary, an unspecified owner format stays unknown; it cannot
support "different format". A needed missing label still follows the main skill's vision path.
